Store & fulfil

Hold stock accurately, then take, promise and pick customer orders across stores, websites and marketplaces.

0%
0 of 7 done6 lessons · quiz · about 6 h
Next lessonWarehouse fulfilment

A warehouse management system (WMS) directs every movement in a warehouse: putaway, storage by bin, lot and expiry, cycle counts, and picking and packing. On the selling side, order-to-cash (O2C) begins: customers get a quote or order on a website, app, store, marketplace or by EDI. Pricing and credit are checked, and the order management system (OMS) promises only what is available (available-to-promise, ATP), reserves it and chooses which node fulfils it: a DC, a store (ship-from-store, BOPIS) or a supplier for drop-ship.

Key idea 1Location accuracy

A WMS knows stock by bin and lot. If the bin is wrong, the picker cannot find it and the promise breaks.

Key idea 2Promise you can keep

Sellable ATP = on hand − reserved − safety stock.

Key idea 3Every store is a warehouse

Omnichannel retailers fulfil web orders from stores as well as DCs.

By the end of this module you can

  • Describe putaway, slotting, cycle counting and FEFO
  • Compare discrete, batch, zone and wave picking
  • Walk through quote → order → credit check → ATP → reservation → sourcing
  • Calculate sellable ATP and a customer's credit exposure
  • Explain BOPIS, BORIS and ship-from-store

Remember these

  1. Store: putaway → bin/lot tracking → cycle counts → pick → pack.
  2. FEFO for perishables; picking is the biggest warehouse labour cost.
  3. O2C starts here: quote → order → price and credit check → ATP → reserve → choose node.
  4. Sellable ATP = on hand − reserved − safety stock; credit exposure = open AR + open orders + new order.
  5. Omnichannel: one stock pool across DCs and stores (ship-from-store, BOPIS, BORIS).

Common mix-ups

✗ Myth: The ERP stock figure is enough to run a warehouse.

✓ Fact: ERP usually knows stock per plant or storage location; a WMS knows the exact bin, pallet and lot needed to pick.

✗ Myth: Always ship from the nearest store.

✓ Fact: Nearest is not always cheapest: pick labour, stock depth and split shipments can make a DC better.

Interview questions

Try answering aloud before opening the model answer.

How would you improve pick productivity?

Slot fast movers near dispatch, choose the right pick method (batch or zone for many small orders), optimise pick paths, use scanning or pick-to-light, and balance labour across waves.

How does an OMS prevent overselling?

It reserves stock atomically when the order is placed, holds back safety stock (especially in stores where counts drift), keeps availability in sync through inventory events, and re-checks at checkout.

What happens if inventory in the WMS and the ERP differ?

Reconcile by location and movement, find the missing or duplicated postings (often failed interface messages), correct with adjustments, and fix the root cause.
