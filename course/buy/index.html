Buy

Turn a need into an approved, legally binding purchase order, the start of procure-to-pay (P2P).

0%
0 of 3 done2 lessons · quiz · about 2.5 h
Next lessonRequisition and purchase order

Buying is where procure-to-pay (P2P) begins. Someone raises a purchase requisition (or planning creates one automatically). It is approved within delegated limits, then converted into a purchase order (PO), which is the company's commitment to the supplier. The supplier acknowledges it. Different needs use different instruments: standard POs, blanket or framework agreements with call-offs, scheduling agreements, consignment and service orders.

Key idea 1The PO is the commitment

A requisition is an internal request. The purchase order is what commits the company to the supplier.

Key idea 2Approve by amount and category

A delegation-of-authority matrix decides who approves which requisition.

Key idea 3Services are different

Services are bought on service POs and confirmed with a service entry sheet instead of a goods receipt.

By the end of this module you can

  • Explain the difference between a requisition and a purchase order
  • Route a requisition through an approval matrix
  • Choose the right purchasing instrument: standard PO, blanket, scheduling agreement, consignment, service PO
  • Name the EDI messages for PO, acknowledgement, ASN and invoice

Remember these

  1. P2P starts with a requisition: need → PR → approval → PO → acknowledgement.
  2. The PO is the external commitment; the PR is internal.
  3. An approval matrix routes requisitions by amount and category.
  4. Pick the instrument to the need: standard, blanket, scheduling agreement, consignment or service PO.
  5. EDI: 850 PO, 855 acknowledgement, 856 ASN, 810 invoice.

Common mix-ups

✗ Myth: Raise the PO after the invoice arrives.

✓ Fact: That is an 'after-the-fact' PO. It removes the approval and price control the PO exists for.

✗ Myth: A requisition commits the company to the supplier.

✓ Fact: Only the purchase order does. Requisitions can be changed or cancelled internally.

Interview questions

Try answering aloud before opening the model answer.

Walk me through procure-to-pay.

Need → purchase requisition → approval → purchase order → supplier acknowledgement → ASN → goods receipt (or service entry) → supplier invoice → two- or three-way match → payment run → reconciliation. Sourcing and contracts (S2C) usually come before it.

When would you use a blanket PO?

For repeated purchases of the same items or services over a period at agreed prices, with call-offs (releases) against it instead of a new PO each time.
