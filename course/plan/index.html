Plan

Next lessonHow a supply chain works

Every supply chain runs the same loop: plan, source, buy, receive, make, store, deliver and settle, then plan again. Goods move downstream from suppliers to customers, money moves upstream, and information (forecasts, orders, notices, invoices) moves both ways. Planning starts the loop: a forecast estimates what customers will buy, sales and operations planning (S&OP) agrees one plan across sales, operations and finance, and inventory policies such as the reorder point and safety stock decide when to replenish.

Key idea 1Three flows

Goods go downstream, money goes upstream, information goes both ways.

Key idea 2Forecasts are always wrong

The aim is a forecast that is less wrong and a plan that copes with the error, using safety stock and short lead times.

Key idea 3Reorder point

Reorder when stock falls to demand during lead time plus safety stock.

By the end of this module you can

  • Name the participants in a supply chain and the three flows between them
  • Explain the eight stages: plan, source, buy, receive, make, store, deliver, settle
  • Explain the path from forecast to S&OP to replenishment
  • Calculate a reorder point with safety stock, and say what EOQ balances

Remember these

  1. A supply chain links suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, retailers and customers.
  2. Goods flow downstream, money upstream, information both ways.
  3. The loop: plan, source, buy, receive, make, store, deliver, settle, then plan again.
  4. Forecast → S&OP → inventory policy → planned orders.
  5. Reorder point = demand during lead time + safety stock; safety stock covers uncertainty.

Common mix-ups

✗ Myth: Logistics and supply chain management are the same thing.

✓ Fact: Logistics (moving and storing goods) is one part of SCM. SCM also covers planning, buying, making, selling and the money.

✗ Myth: A good forecast means we no longer need safety stock.

✓ Fact: Every forecast has error. Safety stock is sized from the size of that error and the lead time.

Interview questions

Try answering aloud before opening the model answer.

Explain the three flows of a supply chain.

Goods move from suppliers towards customers, money moves from customers back towards suppliers, and information such as forecasts, orders, shipping notices and invoices moves in both directions.

What causes the bullwhip effect and how do you reduce it?

Causes: forecasting from orders rather than real demand, order batching, price promotions and shortage gaming. Reduce it by sharing point-of-sale data, VMI or collaborative planning, smaller and more frequent orders, and stable pricing.

How do you calculate safety stock?

A common formula is Z × σ(demand) × √(lead time), where Z sets the service level (for example 1.65 for about 95%). Lead-time variability can be added for unreliable suppliers.

Which forecast accuracy metrics do you know?

MAPE (mean absolute percentage error), weighted MAPE, bias (consistent over- or under-forecasting) and forecast value added.
