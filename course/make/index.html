Make

Turn materials into finished products: bills of materials, MRP, production orders and what the product really cost.

0%
0 of 4 done3 lessons · quiz · about 3 h
Next lessonManufacturing: BOM, routing, MRP and production orders

Manufacturing converts materials into products. A bill of materials (BOM) lists what goes into a product and a routing lists the operations. MRP takes demand, the BOM, stock and lead times and works out what to make or buy and when, creating planned orders. Production orders issue materials, record output and feed costing: inventory is valued at standard cost, moving average or FIFO, including landed costs such as freight and duty. Stock also moves between your own plants and companies, which has its own documents and tax.

Key idea 1MRP explodes the BOM

Demand for a finished product becomes dated requirements for every component, netted against stock.

Key idea 2ERP plans, MES executes

The ERP owns the plan and the cost; the manufacturing execution system runs the shop floor in real time.

Key idea 3Landed cost

Inventory cost = purchase price + freight + duty + insurance + handling.

By the end of this module you can

  • Explain BOM, routing, MRP and production orders
  • Work out net component requirements from a BOM
  • Compare standard cost, moving average and FIFO, and explain landed cost
  • Explain stock transfers between plants and group companies, including GST between GSTINs

3. Which system usually runs real-time shop-floor execution?
4. 100 units bought at ₹10, then 100 at ₹12; 150 sold. What is the FIFO cost of goods sold?
5. Which costs are part of landed cost?

Remember these

  1. BOM = what goes in; routing = how it is made; MRP = what to make or buy, and when.
  2. MRP nets requirements against stock and open orders, then offsets by lead time.
  3. ERP owns the plan and cost; MES runs the floor.
  4. Inventory is valued at standard, moving average or FIFO cost, including landed cost.
  5. Transfers between GSTINs are taxable supplies, even inside one company.

Common mix-ups

✗ Myth: MRP orders whatever the forecast says.

✓ Fact: MRP nets requirements against stock on hand and open orders first, then orders only the shortfall, offset by lead time.

✗ Myth: Inventory cost is the supplier's price.

✓ Fact: Add freight, duty, insurance and handling (landed cost), or margins will look better than they are.

Interview questions

Try answering aloud before opening the model answer.

What is the difference between MRP and DRP?

MRP plans components for production from the bill of materials; DRP plans finished-goods replenishment across a distribution network from DCs to stores.

Standard cost or moving average: which would you choose?

Standard cost suits manufacturing with stable costs and variance analysis; moving average suits trading companies where purchase prices change often. Many ERPs use standard for finished goods and moving average for purchased materials.
