Supply Chain Strategy & Design

Structural choices made once and lived with for years: how centralised to be, what to make or buy, when to customise, and which operating model fits the product.

Centralized vs decentralized supply chains

Who decides, and where inventory and operations sit.

CentralizedDecentralized
DecisionsOne central teamRegions or business units
InventoryFewer, larger DCs; pooled safety stockCloser to customers
StrengthEconomies of scale, consistency, lower total stockResponsiveness, local fit
WeaknessSlower local response, longer last mileDuplication, higher total inventory
FitsStandard products, cost focusDiverse markets, speed focus

Make vs buy decisions

Produce in-house, or outsource? Compare cost, capability, control, capacity and strategic importance.

  • Make when it is a core competence, protects IP, or quality is critical
  • Buy when a supplier has scale or expertise, or demand is volatile
  • Hybrid: make the base load, buy the peaks

Postponement and agile strategies

Delay the final configuration until demand is clearer, so one generic stock serves many variants.

  • Form postponement: final assembly or customisation late (paint tinted in store, printers localised in regional DCs)
  • Time postponement: produce only after the order
  • Place postponement: hold stock centrally and ship only when ordered
  • Lean fits stable, predictable demand; agile fits volatile demand; "leagile" combines them at a decoupling point
