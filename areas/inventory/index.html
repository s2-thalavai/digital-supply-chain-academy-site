Types of inventory

Inventory is classified by its stage and its purpose.

ABC analysis

Rank items by annual consumption value. A few A items carry most of the value and deserve tight control; many C items need only simple rules.

  • A: roughly the top 70–80% of value, often 10–20% of items: count often, forecast carefully
  • B: the next 15–25% of value
  • C: the remaining value from the long tail: simple min/max, count rarely

Inventory costs, formulas and KPIs

Inventory costs money to buy, to order and to hold, and it costs sales when you run out.

  • Holding (carrying) cost: capital, storage, insurance, obsolescence, shrinkage; often estimated at 20–30% of inventory value a year
  • Ordering cost: placing, receiving and paying for an order
  • Stockout cost: lost sales, expediting, customer churn

Top 20 inventory terms

ATP
Available to promise: stock that can still be promised to new orders.
Backorder
Order accepted but not yet fillable from stock.
Cycle count
Counting a subset of locations regularly instead of a full stock-take.
Dead stock
Items with no sales for a long period.
EOQ
Economic order quantity.
FIFO / FEFO
First in first out / first expired first out.
Fill rate
Share of demand met from stock.
Inventory position
On hand + on order − reserved/backordered.
JIT
Just in time: receive materials only as needed.
Lead time
Time from order to availability.
Lot / batch
Group of units produced together, tracked for quality.
MOQ
Minimum order quantity a supplier accepts.
On hand
Physically in stock.
Reorder point
Level that triggers a new order.
Safety stock
Buffer against uncertainty.
Shrinkage
Loss from theft, damage or errors.
SKU
Stock-keeping unit.
Stockout
No stock available to meet demand.
Turnover
How many times inventory is sold and replaced in a period.
VMI
Vendor-managed inventory: the supplier decides replenishment.
