Distribution, Fulfillment & Warehousing

Getting orders out of the building and to the customer: who runs the warehouse, how it is laid out and automated, and what good looks like.

The flow at a glance

1/6

Receive · ASN / GRNASN

ASN · Advance shipping notice / despatch advice. Created when: Goods ship. Triggers: Dock appointment, receiving against ASN. Key fields: Shipment id, PO lines, qty, SSCC per pallet, lot, carrier, ETA.

1PL to 5PL

How much of logistics you outsource, and to whom.

LevelWho does the work
1PLYou: own trucks and warehouses
2PLAsset-based carriers you hire for transport
3PLA provider runs warehousing, transport and value-added services
4PLAn integrator manages several 3PLs and the network on your behalf
5PLNetwork-level aggregation, often for e-commerce; definitions vary

Distribution and fulfillment automation

Automation removes walking, lifting and sorting, the biggest costs in a DC.

  • Conveyors and sortation
  • AS/RS: automated storage and retrieval
  • Goods-to-person and shuttle systems
  • Autonomous mobile robots (AMRs)
  • Pick-to-light, put walls and voice picking
  • Automated packing and labelling
  • A warehouse execution system (WES) orchestrates people and machines

Best practices for distribution centers

What well-run DCs do consistently.

  • Slot fast movers in the "golden zone" near packing and at waist height
  • Cross-dock where possible
  • Cycle count instead of annual shutdowns
  • Dock appointments to smooth inbound
  • Engineered labour standards and real-time productivity
  • Measure dock-to-stock time, order accuracy, units per hour, on-time ship

Warehouse design and layout

Layout decides travel distance, congestion and capacity.

  • Zones: receiving, reserve storage, forward pick, packing, shipping, returns
  • Storage media: selective racking, drive-in, flow racks, mezzanines

U-shapedReceiving and shipping on the same sideMost DCs: shared docks, cross-docking, flexible
