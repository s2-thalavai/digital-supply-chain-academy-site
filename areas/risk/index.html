Risk Management & Resilience

Identifying what could break the chain, reducing the chance and the damage, and recovering fast when it happens.

Supply chain risk management and mitigation

Identify → assess (likelihood × impact) → mitigate → monitor.

Business continuity planning

A plan to keep critical operations running during and after a disruption. ISO 22301 is the common standard.

  1. Business impact analysis. Which products, sites and suppliers matter most; how long can each be down?
  2. Risk assessment. Threats and vulnerabilities per critical process.
  3. Strategies. Alternate suppliers, sites, routes, inventory buffers.
  4. Plans and roles. Who does what, communication trees, decision rights.
  5. Test and maintain. Tabletop exercises and drills; update after every incident.

Cybersecurity in supply chain

Attackers reach companies through their suppliers, software and logistics partners.

  • Third-party risk: suppliers with access to your portals, EDI or networks
  • Software supply chain: compromised updates and libraries (keep SBOMs, scan dependencies)
  • Operational technology in warehouses and plants
  • Controls: MFA everywhere, least privilege, supplier security assessments, segmented networks, tested backups, incident playbooks with partners
