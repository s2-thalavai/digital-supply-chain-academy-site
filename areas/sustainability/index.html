Sustainability & ESG

Measuring and reducing environmental impact, keeping materials in use, and making sure products are made responsibly.

The flow at a glance

1/5

Carbon footprint measurement

Companies report greenhouse gas emissions in three scopes, using the GHG Protocol.

ScopeWhat it coversSupply chain example
Scope 1Direct emissions you ownYour own trucks and forklifts
Scope 2Purchased energyElectricity for warehouses
Scope 3Everything else in your value chainPurchased goods, third-party transport, product use and disposal; often the largest share
MeasureFormulaBasis
Transport emissionstonnes × km × emission factor per tonne-kmuse a recognised framework such as GLEC or your carrier's data

Circular supply chains

Keep products and materials in use for as long as possible instead of take-make-dispose.

  • Reduce, reuse, repair, refurbish, remanufacture, recycle
  • Design for disassembly and recyclability
  • Take-back and returns programmes feed refurbishment
  • Reverse logistics is the operational backbone
BOPIS · BORIS · Ship-from-storep-returns

Ethical sourcing

Making sure suppliers respect human rights, labour standards and the environment.

  • Supplier code of conduct and onboarding questionnaires
  • Audits and certifications
  • Traceability to raw-material origin
  • Grievance mechanisms
  • Regulations to know (requirements change): EU CSRD and CSDDD, Germany's Supply Chain Act, UK Modern Slavery Act, US Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act, India's BRSR reporting
