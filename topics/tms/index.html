Transportation management: planning, tendering, tracking and freight audit

Beginner lens. Share trucks, pick the right carrier, check the bill.

A transportation management system (TMS) plans how orders move: it builds loads, chooses modes and carriers, tenders shipments, books dock appointments, tracks shipments and audits freight invoices before they are paid.

In plain words

Ten orders going to the same city should share one truck. The TMS works that out, asks the cheapest reliable carrier to take it, watches the truck on the way, and checks the carrier's bill against the agreed rate.

Architecture

TMS sits between WMS (ready to ship) and carriers, and sends freight cost to ERP. Tracking data volume is large; publish only milestone and ETA changes.

  • Rates as effective-dated tables by lane, mode, weight break
  • Accessorials pre-approved or flagged
  • Freight accruals at ship, trued up at audit

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
OverbillingBilled weight above shipped weight, wrong rate.
Tender rejectionsCarrier declines; set backup carriers and spot rules.
Duplicate invoicesCarrier resubmits; dedupe on PRO number.
