How a supply chain works

Beginner lens. Remember three flows: goods, information, money. Every system you meet manages one or more of them.

A supply chain is every step that turns a need into a delivered product and a settled payment: plan, buy, make, store, move, sell, and get paid. Three things flow through it at once: goods, information and money. Most enterprise software exists to keep those three flows in step.

In plain words

Think of a neighbourhood bakery. It decides how much bread to bake tomorrow (plan), orders flour (buy), bakes (make), keeps loaves on shelves (store), delivers to cafés (move), sells to you (sell) and collects cash (get paid). A national retailer does exactly this with 50,000 products, 800 suppliers and millions of customers. That scale is why it needs an ERP, an OMS, a WMS and more.

APIs and events

Architecture

Each core system owns one domain and publishes changes. No system edits another system's data directly.

  • ERP owns financial truth (what we owe, what is owed to us)
  • OMS owns the customer promise
  • WMS owns physical stock inside a building
  • Planning owns the future (forecast, plan)
  • An integration layer or event stream connects them

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
Stock shows available but the shelf is emptyInventory record drift: missed scans, theft, unrecorded damage. Fix with cycle counts and safety stock on store inventory.
Supplier paid twiceThe same invoice entered by two channels. Fix with duplicate checks on supplier + invoice number + amount.
Customer promised a date nobody can meetThe OMS promised from stale availability. Fix with real-time inventory events and reservation.
