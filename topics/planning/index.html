Planning, forecasting and replenishment

Planning decides what will be needed and when. Demand planning forecasts sales; supply and inventory planning decide what to buy, make or move; S&OP and IBP align those plans with finance. Execution systems then turn the plan into requisitions, POs and transfers.

In plain words

If an ice-cream shop sells 40 cones a day and the supplier takes 5 days to deliver, it should reorder well before it runs out, keeping a little extra in case of a heatwave. Planning software does this maths for every product in every location.

Architecture

Planning works on snapshots and versions; execution works on live events. The integration is a periodic, reconciled exchange of master data, positions and plans.

  • Planning reads: history, inventory, open POs, lead times
  • Planning writes: forecasts, planned orders, safety stock targets
  • Keep plan versions immutable for S&OP comparison
  • A continuous (s, Q) policy in execution is the safety net between plan runs

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
Forecast ignores stockoutsZero sales on out-of-stock days look like zero demand. Clean history.
Lead times wrong in master dataReorder points too low; frequent stockouts.
Plan never reaches ERPPlanned orders not converted; buyers work from spreadsheets.
