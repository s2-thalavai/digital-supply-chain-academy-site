Advanced planning: replenishment policies, MRP vs DRP, multi-echelon, demand sensing, NPI

Beginner lens. Different items need different ordering rules.

Planning goes beyond one reorder point. Replenishment policies differ (continuous review, periodic review, min/max). MRP plans components from production; DRP plans finished goods through the distribution network. Multi-echelon optimisation decides where to hold stock across tiers. Demand sensing uses very recent signals for the short term, and new product introduction (NPI) forecasts without history.

In plain words

Some shelves are checked every time something sells; others once a week. Factories plan parts (MRP), warehouses plan where finished goods should sit (DRP), and planners guess new products from similar old ones.

Architecture

Choose policy per segment; execution should apply it consistently. Multi-echelon results are parameters (where and how much safety stock), not orders.

  • Periodic review simplifies supplier ordering days
  • Continuous review needs real-time inventory
  • DRP aggregates demand upward; MRP explodes it downward

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
One policy for everythingOverstock on slow items, stockouts on fast.
Double bufferingSafety stock at every tier; use multi-echelon view.
NPI with no planLaunch stockouts; use analogous items.
