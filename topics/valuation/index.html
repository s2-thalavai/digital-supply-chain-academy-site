Inventory valuation and landed cost

Beginner lens. What did the stock really cost? Price plus everything it took to get it here.

Inventory must be valued in the accounts. FIFO assumes the oldest units are used first; weighted average spreads cost across all units; standard cost uses a planned cost and records variances. Landed cost adds freight, insurance, duty and handling to the purchase price so margins are real.

In plain words

You bought 100 pens at ₹10 and later 100 at ₹12, then sold 150. Did they cost ₹10 or ₹12? FIFO, average and standard costing give different answers, and the shipping and duty you paid belong in the cost too.

Architecture

Estimate landed cost at receipt, then true up when actual freight and duty invoices arrive. Keep the allocation basis per charge type.

  • IGST on imports is usually recoverable as input credit, so it is excluded from cost
  • Basic customs duty is a cost
  • Valuation method choice follows accounting policy (Ind AS 2 / IAS 2 do not permit LIFO)

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
Margins overstatedLanded costs ignored.
Late freight invoicesCost revised after goods are sold; accrue estimates.
Wrong allocation basisHeavy cheap items carry too little freight.
