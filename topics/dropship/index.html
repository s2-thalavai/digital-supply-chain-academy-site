Drop-ship and third-party order flows

Beginner lens. Sell it without stocking it.

In drop-shipping, the seller takes the customer order but the supplier ships directly to the customer. The seller never holds the stock. Documents and matching change: there is no warehouse GRN, so the supplier's shipment confirmation or the customer's proof of delivery stands in for receipt.

In plain words

You order a sofa from a furniture website; it arrives straight from the factory. The website sells it to you and buys it from the factory, but never touches it.

Architecture

Drop-ship couples sell-side and buy-side documents tightly. Keep a cross-reference so status and returns flow both ways.

  • US: a drop-shipper may need the seller's resale certificate valid in the delivery state
  • India: bill-to-ship-to rules decide place of supply; e-way bill from supplier to customer
  • No inventory valuation at the seller; margin = sale price − supplier cost

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
No receipt to matchInvoices on hold; use shipment confirmation as receipt.
Tracking not sharedCustomer service blind.
Wrong taxShip-to used instead of bill-to (or the reverse).
