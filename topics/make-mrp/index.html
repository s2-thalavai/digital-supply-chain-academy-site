Manufacturing: BOM, routing, MRP and production orders

Beginner lens. A BOM is a recipe; MRP is working backwards from the delivery date.

Manufacturing turns materials into finished goods. A bill of materials (BOM) lists what goes into a product, a routing lists the operations and work centres, MRP works out what to make or buy and when, and production orders carry the work through the shop floor.

In plain words

A recipe (BOM) says a chair needs one seat, one base and five castors. The method (routing) says assemble, then test. If you must ship 100 chairs in week 6 and assembly takes a week, MRP works backwards: start chairs in week 5, so seats must arrive by week 5, and because seats take two weeks to deliver, order them in week 3.

Architecture

ERP owns the plan and the cost; MES owns execution in real time. Integrate with released orders down and confirmations up, never the other way around.

  • Engineering BOM (PLM) → manufacturing BOM (ERP) with change control
  • MRP output is a proposal; planners firm it
  • Backflushing posts component usage automatically at confirmation
  • Capacity is infinite in classic MRP; use APS for finite scheduling

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
Wrong BOM quantityEvery planned order is wrong. Fix with engineering change control and BOM audits.
Lead times too optimisticComponents arrive late; schedule slips. Measure actual lead times.
Infinite-capacity plansMRP plans more than the work centre can do. Check capacity or use APS.
