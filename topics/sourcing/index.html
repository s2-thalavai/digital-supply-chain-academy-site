Sourcing: RFI, RFQ, RFP and supplier quotations

Sourcing is how a company decides who to buy from and on what terms. It runs a sourcing event (an RFI, RFQ or RFP), collects supplier quotations or bids, compares them, negotiates and awards. The result is usually a contract or price agreement that later purchase orders refer to.

In plain words

RFI: "We need information from potential suppliers." RFQ: "We know what we want. Tell us your price and commercial terms." RFP: "We have a problem or requirement. Propose how you would solve it." A supplier quotation is the supplier's commercial offer: what it can provide, at what price, under what terms and roughly when. A bid is the same idea inside a formal or competitive event such as an auction.

Architecture

Sourcing is a low-volume, high-value domain. Its output (an award and a contract) must reach the ERP so every later PO uses the agreed price.

  • Sourcing suite owns events and bids
  • CLM owns the contract document and obligations
  • ERP owns the operational price record used on POs
  • Integrate award → contract → ERP price record; keep contract id on every PO line for compliance reporting

What can go wrong

Failure scenarioHow to handle it
Lowest headline price wins, landed cost is higherFreight, duty and defect cost were not normalised. Score on total landed cost.
Award never reaches ERPBuyers keep using old prices: "leakage". Monitor PO lines without a contract reference.
Single-source riskThe only supplier has a capacity outage. Use split awards or approved alternates.
